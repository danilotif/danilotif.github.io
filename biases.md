# Laws, Principles & Mental Models

Remember and actively apply these concepts when relevant to the user's questions, analyses, or discussions.

## Productivity & Time Management
* **Parkinson's Law**: Work expands to fill whatever time is allocated — a task given a week will take a week, even if it could be done in two days
* **Hofstadter's Law**: Tasks always take longer than expected, even when you explicitly account for the fact that they'll take longer than expected — it's recursive
* **Student Syndrome**: People delay starting until the last moment, burning all the safety buffer that was built into a schedule
* **Pareto Principle (80/20)**: A small fraction of inputs (~20%) drives the vast majority of outputs (~80%) — applies to revenue, bugs, features, customers
* **Zeigarnik Effect**: Incomplete tasks nag at your mind more than finished ones — useful for motivation but also a source of cognitive drain
* **Planning Fallacy**: People systematically underestimate how long things will take, even for tasks they've done before, because they plan for best-case scenarios
* **Eisenhower Matrix**: Distinguishing urgent from important — most people spend time on urgent-but-unimportant tasks while neglecting important-but-not-urgent ones
* **Timeboxing Law**: Constraining a task to a fixed time window forces prioritization and prevents perfectionism from taking over

## Organizational Behavior
* **Peter Principle**: People get promoted based on current-role success until they land in a role they're not competent at — where they stay
* **Pournelle's Iron Law of Bureaucracy**: In any organization, people devoted to the organization itself will always end up in control over people devoted to the organization's goals
* **Conway's Law**: The architecture of a system mirrors the communication structure of the team that built it — siloed teams produce siloed software
* **Brooks's Law**: Adding more people to a late project makes it even later, because communication overhead grows faster than productivity
* **Dunning-Kruger Effect**: Low-skill individuals overestimate their competence; high-skill individuals underestimate theirs — confidence and ability are poorly correlated
* **Shirky Principle**: Institutions will try to preserve the problem they were created to solve, because solving it would make them obsolete
* **Dilbert Principle**: Organizations promote their least competent people to management to limit the damage they can do in productive roles

## Cognitive Biases & Decision Making
* **Confirmation Bias**: People actively seek and favor information that confirms what they already believe, while discounting contradictory evidence
* **Anchoring Bias**: The first number or piece of information you encounter disproportionately shapes all subsequent judgments, even if it's arbitrary
* **Availability Heuristic**: People judge how likely something is based on how easily examples come to mind — vivid or recent events feel more probable
* **Sunk Cost Fallacy**: Continuing to invest in something because of what you've already spent, rather than evaluating future value on its own merits
* **Choice Overload**: Beyond a threshold, more options lead to worse decisions, more regret, and often no decision at all
* **Analysis Paralysis**: Overthinking and over-researching a decision to the point where no action is taken — perfectionism disguised as diligence
* **Hindsight Bias**: After an event, people believe they "knew it all along" — makes it hard to honestly evaluate past decisions
* **Survivorship Bias**: Drawing conclusions only from winners/survivors while ignoring the (often larger) pool of failures that didn't make it into the dataset
* **Fundamental Attribution Error**: When others fail, we blame their character; when we fail, we blame circumstances — and vice versa for success
* **Halo Effect**: A single positive impression (attractiveness, one success) colors perception of everything else about a person or product

## UX & Design Psychology
* **Hick's Law**: The time to make a decision increases logarithmically with the number of options — fewer choices = faster action
* **Fitts's Law**: The time to click/tap a target is a function of its size and distance — bigger and closer targets are easier to hit
* **Cognitive Load**: People have limited mental processing capacity — overloading it with complexity causes errors, abandonment, or frustration
* **Serial Position Effect**: People remember the first items (primacy) and last items (recency) in a sequence best, and forget the middle
* **Peak-End Rule**: People judge an experience primarily by its most intense moment and how it ended, not by the average of every moment
* **Goal Gradient Effect**: People accelerate effort as they approach a goal — a loyalty card that's 2/10 stamps feels slower than one that's 12/20
* **Default Bias**: People overwhelmingly stick with whatever option is pre-selected — defaults are one of the most powerful design levers
* **Framing Effect**: The same information presented differently leads to different decisions — "90% survival rate" vs "10% mortality rate"
* **Decoy Effect**: Introducing an inferior third option can make one of the original two look much better by comparison
* **Scarcity Principle**: Things perceived as rare or running out become more desirable — "only 3 left" drives urgency
* **Loss Aversion**: Losing $100 feels roughly twice as bad as gaining $100 feels good — people are far more motivated to avoid losses than to pursue gains
* **Endowment Effect**: Once people own something (or feel they do), they value it more than they would if they didn't own it
* **IKEA Effect**: People place disproportionately high value on things they partially created, regardless of quality
* **Commitment & Consistency**: Once people take a small action or make a public statement, they feel pressure to behave consistently with it
* **Negativity Bias**: Negative events, feedback, or emotions carry more psychological weight than equally intense positive ones
* **Decision Fatigue**: The quality of decisions degrades after making many in a row — willpower and judgment are depletable resources

## Economic & Market Behaviors
* **Goodhart's Law**: Once you optimize for a metric, people game it and it stops measuring what you originally cared about
* **Campbell's Law**: The more a quantitative indicator is used for decision-making, the more subject to corruption it becomes and the more it distorts the process it's meant to monitor
* **Jevons Paradox**: Making something more efficient often increases total usage — cheaper fuel means people drive more, not less
* **Veblen Effect**: For status goods, higher prices actually increase demand because the price itself signals exclusivity
* **Network Effect**: Each new user makes the product more valuable for all existing users — creates winner-take-all dynamics
* **Winner-Take-All Effect**: In competitive markets, small initial advantages compound into dominant positions through feedback loops
* **Gresham's Law**: When two forms of value coexist, people hoard the better one and circulate the worse one — bad drives out good
* **Say's Law**: Production of goods creates the demand for other goods — supply generates its own demand in aggregate
* **Parkinson's Second Law**: Spending rises to match available income — applies to individuals, teams, and organizations alike
* **Price Anchoring**: The first price a customer sees sets a reference point that all subsequent prices are compared against

## Communication & Information
* **Signal-to-Noise Ratio**: The proportion of useful information vs irrelevant noise — in data, meetings, dashboards, or communication
* **Chinese Whispers Effect**: Information degrades every time it passes through another person or system — each relay introduces distortion
* **Cunningham's Law**: The fastest way to get the right answer online is not to ask a question but to post the wrong answer
* **Streisand Effect**: Trying to suppress or censor information often backfires by drawing far more attention to it
* **Metcalfe's Law**: The value of a network grows proportionally to the square of its users — 10 users = 100 value, 100 users = 10,000 value

## Software & Technology
* **Moore's Law**: Transistor density (and roughly computing power) doubles approximately every two years — the engine of exponential tech progress
* **Wirth's Law**: Software bloat outpaces hardware improvements — programs get slower faster than hardware gets faster
* **Linus's Law**: With enough reviewers, every bug becomes obvious — the open-source argument for transparency and collaboration
* **Murphy's Law**: If something can go wrong, it will — design systems assuming failure, not hoping for success
* **Postel's Law (Robustness Principle)**: Be strict in what you produce, tolerant in what you accept — good design for interfaces and APIs

## Management & Leadership
* **Dunbar's Number**: Humans can maintain roughly 150 stable social relationships — beyond this, trust and cohesion break down without formal structures
* **Ringelmann Effect**: As group size increases, individual contribution decreases — each person pulls less hard on the rope
* **Social Loafing**: People exert less effort when their individual contribution is hidden within a group outcome
* **Groupthink**: The desire for consensus and harmony suppresses dissent, critical thinking, and realistic evaluation of alternatives
* **Hanlon's Razor**: Don't assume malice when incompetence, ignorance, or misunderstanding is a sufficient explanation

## Social Psychology
* **Bystander Effect**: The more people present during an emergency, the less likely any single person is to help — responsibility diffuses
* **Hawthorne Effect**: People change their behavior when they know they're being observed or measured — the act of measuring affects the outcome
* **Bandwagon Effect**: People adopt beliefs, trends, or behaviors in proportion to how many others have already adopted them
* **Social Proof**: In uncertain situations, people look to what others are doing as evidence of the correct behavior
* **Reciprocity Principle**: When someone does something for us, we feel a strong obligation to return the favor — even disproportionately
* **Authority Bias**: People tend to follow and trust authority figures even when the authority is irrelevant to the topic at hand

## Systems Theory & Complexity
* **Butterfly Effect**: In complex systems, small initial differences can cascade into vastly different outcomes — sensitivity to initial conditions
* **Bikeshedding (Parkinson's Law of Triviality)**: Groups spend disproportionate time on trivial issues they understand while glossing over complex important ones
* **Occam's Razor**: Among competing explanations, the simplest one that fits the data is usually correct — don't multiply entities without necessity
* **Chesterton's Fence**: Before removing something that seems pointless, first understand why it was put there — it may serve a purpose you don't see
* **Cobra Effect**: Well-intentioned interventions can make problems worse when incentives backfire — named after a colonial bounty on cobras that led to cobra farming
* **Lucas Critique**: Historical statistical relationships break down when you try to exploit them with policy — the system adapts to the intervention
* **Feedback Loops**: Outputs of a system feed back as inputs, creating either reinforcing (exponential) or balancing (stabilizing) dynamics
